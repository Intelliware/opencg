# Juniper Bay Group AML/ATF Programme Summary

Owner: Chief Anti-Money Laundering Officer (CAMLO)
Applies to: Juniper Bay Financial Group (Wealth, Payments, Bank)
Classification: Internal
Version: 3.0, Effective: 2026-01-01

## Purpose

This summary describes how Juniper Bay Financial Group manages the risk of money laundering and terrorist financing. It is a high-level description for staff. Detailed procedures belong to each subsidiary, and detection parameters are held in restricted documents owned by the relevant line of business. The summary is illustrative and is not legal or regulatory guidance.

## Programme structure

The programme has five components, applied consistently across the group:

1. Governance: a designated officer, board oversight and written policies.
2. Risk assessment: a documented enterprise assessment of clients, products, channels and geographies, reviewed each year.
3. Client due diligence: identity verification and understanding of the client relationship (KYC).
4. Ongoing monitoring: review of activity against what is expected for the client.
5. Reporting, training and independent testing.

The programme reflects obligations under Canada's PCMLTFA and is overseen from a regulatory standpoint by FINTRAC. Prudential matters for the bank also involve OSFI.

## The CAMLO role

The Chief Anti-Money Laundering Officer (CAMLO) is appointed by the Board of Juniper Bay Financial Group and reports to the Chief Risk Officer, with a direct line to the Board Risk Committee. The CAMLO:

- owns this programme and approves subsidiary procedures;
- approves the annual enterprise risk assessment;
- decides whether a matter is escalated for external reporting;
- receives a quarterly report from each subsidiary's AML lead;
- ensures staff training is completed each year.

Each subsidiary has an AML Lead who reports functionally to the CAMLO.

## Know your client (KYC)

Before a relationship starts, staff verify identity using approved methods and record the purpose of the relationship. Clients are placed in one of three risk ratings: low, medium or high. High-risk clients need senior approval and enhanced due diligence. Clients who cannot be verified are not onboarded.

KYC records are refreshed on a schedule tied to risk: every 3 years for low, every 2 years for medium and every year for high.

## Ongoing monitoring

Monitoring is risk-based. Systems and staff look for activity that does not fit what is known about the client. Alerts are reviewed by the subsidiary's AML team, and unresolved concerns are escalated to the AML Lead. Specific scenarios, thresholds and tuning are owned by each line of business and are not described here.

## Reporting to FINTRAC

When staff have reasonable grounds to suspect that a transaction or attempted transaction is related to money laundering or terrorist financing, they raise an internal unusual activity report to the AML Lead. The CAMLO decides on external reporting to FINTRAC. Staff must never tell a client that a report has been or may be made. Other prescribed reports are filed by the AML team under the subsidiary procedures.

## How each subsidiary applies the programme

- **Juniper Bay Wealth** applies KYC at account opening and at each suitability review. Advisors escalate source-of-funds concerns to the Wealth AML Lead.
- **Juniper Bay Payments** screens payments and counterparties, and monitors transaction patterns. Payment screening and fraud rules are owned by Payments and restricted to that line of business.
- **Juniper Bay Bank** applies KYC at branch and digital onboarding, and monitors deposit, lending and card activity. Branch staff escalate through the Retail AML Lead.

## Records and training

Records of identity verification, risk ratings and internal reports are kept for at least 7 years. All staff complete AML training at hire and each year after. Roles with higher exposure complete additional training every 6 months.
