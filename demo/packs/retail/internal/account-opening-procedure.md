# Retail Account Opening and Credit Application Procedure

Owner: Head of Branch Operations
Applies to: Juniper Bay Bank
Classification: Internal
Version: 4.1
Effective: 2026-02-01

This procedure tells branch and contact-centre staff how to open personal accounts, take credit applications, and handle fee and rate requests. It applies the *Juniper Bay Group AML/ATF Programme Summary* and the *Juniper Bay Group Data Classification Standard*. It depends on the *Retail Credit Decisioning Policy* (Confidential) and the *Retail Pricing Exception Authorities* (Confidential) for the specifics noted below.

## 1. Before you start

- Confirm the customer is present, or that the remote identity process is complete.
- Use the account-opening workflow in the branch platform. Do not open accounts on paper except during a documented outage.
- Never record identification numbers in free-text notes.

## 2. Identity verification and risk

1. Verify identity using two independent methods from the approved list. The *Juniper Bay Group AML/ATF Programme Summary* describes the purpose and the escalation path.
2. Record the purpose of the account and the expected activity.
3. Ask whether the customer is, or is related to, a politically exposed person. If yes, stop and refer to the AML team before opening the account.
4. If anything about the customer's story does not add up, do not tip the customer off. Complete an unusual transaction report internally and follow the AML escalation path.

## 3. Opening the account

1. Select the product that matches the customer's needs. Offer Student Chequing to eligible students.
2. Explain fees using the *Juniper Bay Bank Chequing and Savings Account Disclosures*. Make sure the customer understands how fees can be waived.
3. Explain overdraft protection only if the customer asks, or if the customer's circumstances suggest it would help. Follow the *Juniper Bay Bank Overdraft Protection Terms*. Do not pre-select overdraft.
4. Obtain consents, signatures and the account agreement through the workflow.
5. Issue the card and set up online banking.

## 4. Taking a credit application

Staff take applications for personal loans, lines of credit and credit cards. Staff do not decide them.

- Complete the application in the workflow and capture income and debt details accurately.
- Do not tell the customer whether they will be approved, and do not quote a score. The decision comes from the decision engine.
- The auto-decline score cut-off, the debt-service ratio limits and the referral rules are set out in the *Retail Credit Decisioning Policy* (Confidential). This procedure does not repeat them. If you need them to do your job, you will have access to that document.
- When the engine returns a refer outcome, send the file to the Retail Credit Underwriting team the same day.
- When an application is declined, give the customer the decline notice generated by the workflow. Do not paraphrase the reason.

## 5. Fee waivers and rate discount requests

Customers sometimes ask for a fee waived or a lower rate. Staff may offer published waivers, such as those in the account disclosures, without approval.

For anything beyond published waivers, including a discount on an advertised loan or mortgage rate, the amount you can approve depends on your role. How much of a rate discount a branch manager may approve, and the other approval levels, are set out in the *Retail Pricing Exception Authorities* (Confidential). Do not promise a customer an exception before it is approved and recorded in the workflow. Record the reason for every exception.

## 6. Complaints

If a customer raises a complaint at any point, log it according to the *Juniper Bay Group Complaint-Handling Standard*.

## 7. Quality checks

The branch manager reviews a sample of opened accounts each month for completeness, identity evidence and correct disclosure. Findings go to the Head of Branch Operations quarterly.
